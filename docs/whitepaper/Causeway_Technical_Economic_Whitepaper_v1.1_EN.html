01 / Executive Summary

Executive Summary

Causeway is a market intelligence layer for prediction markets. Its core thesis is that prediction markets are not isolated pages; they are networks of probability shaped by real-world events. A policy signal, sports result, company announcement, legal filing, or on-chain event rarely affects only one market. It casts a market shadow across related narratives, assets, and participant expectations.

Current prediction-market interfaces expose prices, titles, volume, and rules, but they do not provide the reasoning layer between an event and a reviewable decision. Users need structured inference: what may be affected, through which path, with what confidence, which assumptions remain unverified, and which actions should be previewed rather than executed immediately.

Causeway's role: Causeway is not an autonomous betting bot. It is a system for event reasoning, market mapping, source verification, simulation, previews, and user-governed decision workflows.

Version 1.1 expands the original technical-economic whitepaper with three major additions: the market-shadow thesis, a five-phase roadmap from market data to optional delegated execution, and the long-term vision of a swarm intelligence prediction engine that turns multi-agent simulations into professional prediction reports.

02 / Market Thesis

Market Problem and Product Thesis

2.1 The Interface Gap

Prediction-market prices aggregate expectations, but most interfaces remain market-by-market. For complex events, the real user problem is cross-market: did the event change related probabilities, has that change already been priced in, which markets lag, and which links are merely narrative noise?

2.2 Causeway's Principles

Principle 01Every event has a market shadow

A single event can ripple through policy, macro, sports, crypto, election, and company narratives.

Principle 02Reasoning must be visible

Useful AI should expose assumptions, confidence, uncertainty, and the reasoning path behind each market connection.

Principle 03Speed needs governance

Fast reaction is valuable only when preview, confirmation, audit, and permission boundaries remain clear.

2.3 Target Outcome

Causeway organizes event sensing, market mapping, AI reasoning, source verification, action previews, and user confirmation into one workflow. The near-term objective is better prediction-market analysis and previews. The mid-term objective is a verifiable intelligence layer. The long-term objective is a swarm intelligence engine capable of simulating how the world may move.

04 / Architecture

System Architecture

4.1 Six Layers

  1. Market Data Foundation: a normalized, queryable, auditable market data base.
  2. Market Graph: relationships based on categories, semantics, historical behavior, liquidity, and AI reasoning.
  3. Reasoning Engine: multi-layer impact paths, scores, explanations, and review states.
  4. Source Object Layer: normalized objects for news, filings, chain data, sports data, and official releases.
  5. Verification Layer: authority-source checks, conflict detection, and freshness validation.
  6. Governed Preview & Execution: previews, constraints, audit records, and user confirmation.

05 / Reasoning

AI Reasoning and Market Shadows

Causeway's AI layer should not merely produce an answer. It should produce a reviewable market-shadow graph that describes which markets are affected, how they are connected, why the link matters, how confident the system is, and what remains uncertain.

5.1 Scoring Dimensions

Relevance

Whether the event has a clear entity, theme, or economic relation to the market.

Direction

Whether the event supports, opposes, hedges, contradicts, or correlates with a market path.

Confidence

The strength of evidence, comparability, and source quality.

Tradability

Whether the market is active, liquid, understandable, and previewable.

5.2 From Single Model to Agent Teams

Early versions may rely on a single model for structured outputs. Later versions should introduce agents with roles: analyst, skeptic, verifier, risk officer, and report writer. The result becomes a collective reasoning trace with visible consensus and conflict.

06 / Verification

Source Objects and Verification

Speed is not useful if the source is wrong. Causeway's verification layer traces claims back to primary or authoritative sources: official releases, regulatory filings, sports-league data, court documents, government databases, company statements, and on-chain records.

FieldPurpose
originThe original information source.
claimA concrete factual claim to verify.
timestampPublished, fetched, and last-updated time.
entitiesPeople, organizations, markets, assets, teams, or regions.
confidenceA combined source-quality, freshness, and conflict score.
rawPayloadThe original data retained for audit and replay.

Verification should affect workflow state. Low-confidence claims enter verify mode. Conflicting claims trigger warnings. High-impact but unverified claims may only enter dry-run preview. Expired sources require refresh.

08 / Roadmap

Five-Phase Roadmap

Phase 01Now

Market Data Foundation

Phase one focuses on Polymarket market data first. External sources are a later source-object layer. The system must understand market structure, prices, liquidity, books, rules, and state before ingesting external noise.

Phase 02Reasoning

Reasoning Model

Build a stronger model that maps one event into all relevant markets and scores relevance, direction, confidence, risk, and suggested review action.

Phase 03Real-time

Real-Time Scenario Generation

Ingest real-time news streams and generate cross-market scripts: what happened, which themes matter, which markets are affected, and which confirmation signals are missing.

Phase 04Verification

Source Verification Layer

Before action, trace claims to primary sources, detect conflicts, check freshness, and reduce hallucination and misinformation risk.

Phase 05Future

Delegated AI Execution

Users may optionally delegate bounded authority to AI under explicit limits: market categories, order size, loss budget, data-source requirements, expiry, revocation, and audit.

09 / Governance

Governance, Risk, and Control

  • AI may reason, rank, simulate, and preview, but should not bypass user confirmation.
  • Every real action requires a preview with path, risk checks, limits, and audit summary.
  • The system must distinguish dry-run, guarded preview, user-confirmed action, and delegated execution.
  • Delegation must be optional, bounded, revocable, expiring, and auditable.
RiskControl

11 / Compliance Boundary

Data, Audit, and Compliance Boundary

Causeway should be designed as workflow software, a market intelligence system, and a user-governed decision tool. It should not be framed as a return-guaranteeing system or default autonomous trader.

  • No AI output should be framed as investment advice.
  • No prediction, strategy, or market outcome should be guaranteed.
  • Simulation, preview, and real action must remain clearly distinct.
  • Delegated execution requires explicit permission, scope, budget, expiry, and revocation.
